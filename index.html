About

How AI adoption reorganizes work, and whether control holds

I study what happens to the organization of work when a general-purpose technology arrives. When AI assistance enters an open production system, participation is redistributed rather than simply increased: some contributors renew and others leave, the mix of demand shifts, authorship separates from review, and the controls an organization believed it had start to behave differently. I measure those shifts directly from platform event logs (33 million events across 65 GitHub repositories in the current study), using quasi-experimental causal inference, network models, and blinded LLM instruments that read evidence out of code review rather than relying on timestamps alone. Four years as a systems and processes analyst before the PhD taught me that a model only matters if someone can make a decision with it.

I am on the academic job market for faculty positions starting in 2027.
